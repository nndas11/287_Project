Introduction to Neural Networks

Neural networks are computational models loosely inspired by the structure of the
human brain. They consist of layers of interconnected nodes, commonly called
neurons or perceptrons, that process and transform input data.

The first layer is the input layer, which receives raw data such as pixel values,
text tokens, or numerical features. The output layer produces the final result,
which may be a classification label, a probability score, or a continuous value.

[Note: Detailed descriptions of hidden layers, activation functions (ReLU, sigmoid,
tanh), weight initialization strategies, and the backpropagation algorithm are
contained in Sections 3–7, which are not included in this text excerpt.]

Training a neural network involves adjusting the weights of connections between
neurons so that the network's predictions become more accurate over time. This
process is guided by a loss function that measures the gap between predicted and
actual outputs.
